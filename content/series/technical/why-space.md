## Overview

*Why Space* is the economic case for space mass transit, written for the general reader. It is the standalone companion to the three-volume *Orbital Ring Engineering*. Before the engineering matters, the economics have to add up: what does it cost to move a kilogram to low Earth orbit today, what is the floor set by physics, and what is the gap? This book walks through that gap and the handful of technologies with any realistic chance of closing it.

The intent is that a reader with no prior aerospace background can follow the argument. The math stays conversational. Where equations appear, they are developed from physical intuition first.

## What's Inside

- Apollo, the lost decades and the new space race: what the first space age accomplished, where the institutions failed, and what capabilities are now emerging.
- The cost-per-kilogram problem: where today's prices come from, and why the floor is higher than it looks.
- The alternatives on the table: chemical rockets, skyhooks, mass drivers, launch loops, space fountains, space elevators, orbital rings.
- How each option scales with demand: what happens when annual launch mass goes up by 10x, 100x, 1000x.
- Financing and construction: the capital stack for multi-trillion-dollar infrastructure, and why the usual objection misses the point.
- The comparison matrix: a single table summarizing every option on cost, risk, throughput, and time-to-operation.
