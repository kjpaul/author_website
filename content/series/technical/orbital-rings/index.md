A three-volume engineering treatment of the orbital ring: a cable in orbit around the Earth, moving faster than orbital velocity, with a stationary casing riding on it. Anchor lines tie the casing to the ground, a mass driver on the ring launches spacecraft, and space elevators bring people home.

## What the series covers

The idea goes back to the 1870s. The modern engineering treatment dates from 1982. The materials science is on a credible development path. What has not existed until now is a single engineering reference that takes one ring as a whole, sizes what it can, and lists what it has not.

This three-volume series is that reference.

- **Volume I: Space Mass Transit Complete Design Review.** A full design review of an integrated orbital ring with mass driver and space elevator. Materials, power, control, orbital mechanics, construction sequencing, failure modes. The volume to read first.

- **Volume II: First Principles: Orbital Mechanics, Materials Science, and Electrical Systems.** The groundwork the series builds on, derived from scratch. Written at the level of an undergraduate textbook. Every result is derived. Every derivation is shown.

- **Volume III: Mass Transit Launch Technologies: Levitation, Linear Motors, Mass Drivers, and Space Elevators.** The systems of the ring in detail: the magnetic levitation that carries the casing, the linear motors that deploy the ring, the mass driver, and the space elevators.

The economic case is a separate book. *Why Space: Apollo, the Lost Decades, the New Space Race, and the Infrastructure Worth Building* is the standalone companion to the series, written for a general educated reader: why an orbital ring, what rockets can and cannot do, what alternative launch systems exist, and how the economics close. Math is light and conversational; every equation that appears is explained in words first. [More about Why Space](/series/related/why-space/).

A further volume on construction logistics is planned for after the companion fiction series is complete.

## Where to start

Volume I, the design review, is the volume to read first, and the one the open-source code and the published references serve. *Why Space*, the economic case, is the starting point for the general reader. It is planned for mid-2027, after the THOR Drive volumes.

## What makes this series different

The series is written in the tradition of Gerard K. O'Neill's *The High Frontier* and Robert Zubrin's *The Case for Mars*: rigorous enough that the numbers can be checked, readable enough that a motivated non-specialist can follow the argument.

What sets it apart is the math. O'Neill wrote in 1976, before carbon nanotubes and before reusable rockets. This series adds a complete engineering treatment, with every number derived or cited.

The simulation code is open source. The references for every factual claim are published online. Errata are publicly tracked. If a number is wrong, the author wants to know.

## Companion science fiction series

The technical works are paired with the five hard science fiction novels of *Astronomy's Shocking Twist*. The fiction and the technical volumes share a single universe grounded in the same physics. The novels tell a story that could happen, using the infrastructure the technical books describe. Other science fiction authors are welcome to use this material in their own work.

The five novels are planned and titled; the titles follow the story outline and are not final.
